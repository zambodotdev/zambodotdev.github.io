The flagship

Work ends in a handover, not a dead chat.

A chat ends and the work evaporates: what was decided, what state things are in, who owns what. A handover is the opposite. The job wraps with its summary, its goal, its next steps, and every receipt from the job chained inside it, addressed to a named recipient: a client, a teammate, or the next AI.

  • Pickup is always free, in any AI, and handovers do not expire.
  • The recipient can check the work: every receipt recomputes, so "it is done" is verified, not believed.
  • Built for people who hand work to clients: the delivery record, the sign-off, and the answer to "what changed since delivery" live in one place.

The standard

AER-1: receipts anyone can recompute.

Receipts only settle arguments if everyone computes them the same way. AER-1 is that shared computation: an IETF Internet-Draft (draft-zambo-aer1-26), a frozen public corpus, and verifiers run by people who did not write it.

The corpus

143 vectors · 146 checks

The conformance corpus is public and frozen under one digest: sha256 60bf73f3…2db0d. Independent agents re-fetch and recompute it in public; on Nov 14 one recomputed all 72 corpus files, 72 of 72 hashes matching.

The registry

Independent verifiers

Five independent validators are listed in the public registry, including WhiteMagic Labs and Laurent Lemonnier. Their scores are theirs: the registry reports what each verifier actually ran.

The challenge

Run the kit yourself

The conformance challenge is open on GitHub: take the kit, run the corpus, publish your score. The standard does not ask to be trusted. It asks to be run.

Maintained in public

Today's errata

On Nov 15 an outside team published two tensions they found between the draft text and the corpus. Both were resolved from the draft's own text and shipped as a public errata the same day. No corpus result changed.

Why this exists at all: "Everyone needs Zambo receipts", the short essay on the gap between an AI saying the work is done and anyone being able to check it.
